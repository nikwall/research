Management and Performance in the Public Sector: Evidence from German Municipalities

Florian Englmaier, Gerd Muehlheusser, Andreas Roider, Matthias Scherr and Niklas Wallmeier

Working paper

October 2026. A substantial revision of IZA DP 15676, CEPR DP 17670 and CESifo WP 10060 (2022); preregistered at the Open Science Foundation.

PDFIZA DP 15676 (2022)PreregistrationCite

In short

A management survey of 600 German town halls. How they are run varies widely, even within the same state, and those with more structured management perform better, from finances and attractiveness to online services.

What we find

  • We adapted the World Management Survey to local government and surveyed the heads of 600 municipalities, representative of those with more than 5,000 inhabitants, on goal setting, monitoring of service quality, organizational learning, structured processes, human resources and digitization.
  • Municipalities differ widely: the management score at the 75th percentile is almost 1.5 standard deviations above the one at the 25th, and the differences persist within states, regional types and size brackets.
  • Structured management is rare: only 20 percent track indicators of service quality, and half of all municipalities follow an almost purely “administered” style. Few are “actively managed” across all six areas.
  • Better management goes with better performance: moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of the score goes with 14 percent more net migration and a 19 percent larger business tax base, measured against the mean, and with more online services.
  • Monitoring of service quality and human resource management matter most. Overall, municipalities use structured management less intensively than private manufacturing firms in Germany.
