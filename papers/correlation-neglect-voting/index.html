Correlation Neglect in Voting Decisions: An Experiment

Johannes Moser and Niklas Wallmeier

Economics Letters 198, 109656

JournalCite

In short

An experiment on whether voters account for the correlation between the pieces of information they receive.

What we find

  • Members of a group vote for a fixed payment or for a lottery with the same expected value. Signals hint at which option may earn the group a bonus or a penalty.
  • Each voter has to weigh these signals against her own attitude to risk.
  • Correlation neglect, treating signals from a shared source as if they were independent, makes voters flip their vote.
  • The bias improves efficiency: the groups end up better off.
